How it works

  1. Each past appointment is marked attended or missed. Ordinary cancellations are left out; late cancellations count as missed unless you untick that box.
  2. No-show rates are worked out by day, time, how far ahead it was booked, service, channel, reminder, new versus returning, and the customer's own track record (using only visits before each appointment). Groups with fewer than 30 appointments are flagged as "few" — don't act on them alone.
  3. With enough history (150+ appointments and 15+ no-shows) a logistic regression is trained right here in your browser on the oldest 75% of appointments and tested on the newest 25%. The AUC score says how well it ranks risk (0.5 = coin toss, 1.0 = perfect). It is then retrained on everything to score upcoming bookings.
  4. With less history, a simple, transparent score is used instead: your overall no-show rate multiplied up or down by how each factor's rate compares, with small groups pulled towards average.
  5. Reasons shown next to each booking are the factors pushing its risk up the most.
  6. Overbooking: for each day, the chance of 0, 1, 2… no-shows is worked out from the individual risks. The suggestion is the most extra bookings where the chance that everyone turns up anyway (a clash) stays under your caution level.

Limits: this predicts likelihood, not certainty — a "high risk" customer usually still turns up. Never refuse service based on a score; use it to decide who gets an extra reminder or a deposit request. The tool can't send SMS for you or run on a schedule; download the list (or calendar tasks) and use your booking system's reminders.
