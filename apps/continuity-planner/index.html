Business Continuity Planner

Answer guided questions about what keeps your business running. Get a risk register, business impact analysis, contact tree, step-by-step incident checklists, a testing schedule and a printable continuity and disaster recovery plan.

Everything stays in this browser. Download a backup and keep a printed copy of the finished plan somewhere other than your premises.

Start with the basics. The incident coordinator is the person who takes charge when something goes wrong (often the owner).

What must keep running for the business to survive? Think about what earns money, what customers would notice first, and legal deadlines (payroll, tax). For each one: the maximum tolerable downtime (the longest you could survive without it), the RTO (how quickly you aim to have it back — shorter than the maximum), and the RPO (how much recent data you could afford to lose and re-enter).

Who does work that others can't easily cover? Tick "key person" and name a deputy who could step in. These names build the contact tree.

Which suppliers, software providers or contractors could stop you working if they failed? Note an alternative for each critical one.

List every system that holds business data — accounting, email, files, booking or job software, the website. How is each backed up, how often, is a copy kept offsite or in the cloud, and when did you last check you could restore it?
