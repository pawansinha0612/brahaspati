How it works

  1. Sentiment: every word is scored from a list of about 400 review words (for example "rude" −3, "lovely" +3). "Not", "never" and "didn't" flip the next few words, "very" and "extremely" strengthen them, and the part after "but" counts more. When there is a star rating it counts for 60% of the verdict.
  2. Themes: each sentence is checked for theme words (wait, staff, price…) and for clear complaint patterns like "waited 40 minutes" or "runs small". A sentence counts as a complaint about a theme when it is negative or matches a complaint pattern.
  3. Recurring complaints are ranked by how many reviews complain × how strongly.
  4. Emerging issues: the share of reviews complaining about a theme in the last two months compared with the four months before. A theme is flagged when it has grown by half or more (and at least 3 reviews).
  5. Phrases: two and three-word phrases that keep coming up in negative (or positive) reviews, to catch things the themes miss.
  6. Reply drafts are built from the complaint themes found in each review. Always read and personalise before posting.

Limits: word-based scoring misses sarcasm, slang and mixed languages, and short reviews ("meh") carry little signal. It can't fetch reviews from Google or Amazon for you — a browser isn't allowed to read other websites — so export or copy them in. Use the numbers as a guide and read the quotes.
