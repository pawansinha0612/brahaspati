How the forecast works

  1. Sales are added up by week (Monday to Sunday) for each product. Weeks with no sales count as zero.
  2. Up to six methods are tried: 4, 8 and 13-week averages, exponential smoothing, Holt's trend method and, when there are at least two years of history, Holt-Winters with a yearly season.
  3. Each method is trained on all but the last few weeks and tested on those hidden weeks. The method with the smallest error wins and is retrained on all the data.
  4. The forecast range (shaded) is a 90% range based on how wrong the method was week to week.
  5. Safety stock = z × weekly forecast error × √(lead time in weeks), where z comes from your service level (1.64 for 95%).
  6. Reorder point = forecast demand during the lead time + safety stock. When stock falls to this level, order.
  7. Order quantity tops you up to the reorder point plus the weeks of cover you chose, or uses the economic order quantity √(2 × yearly demand × cost per order ÷ holding cost per unit) if you tick it.
  8. ABC: A items make the first 80% of the last 52 weeks' sales value, B the next 15%, C the rest.
  9. Dead stock: no sales for 13 weeks. Slow: selling under a quarter of its usual rate, or more than 26 weeks of stock on hand.

Limits: a forecast is only as good as the history. New products, promotions, stock-outs (which hide real demand) and one-off bulk orders all fool it — use your judgement on top. Nothing here runs on a schedule; re-import fresh sales each week or month.
