How it works

  1. Each return is matched to its order line by order ID and SKU, so it picks up the category, size, channel and customer.
  2. Return rate = units returned ÷ units sold, by product, size, category, channel and order month.
  3. Cost of a return = refund + return shipping + handling + write-off (cost price of items that can't be resold).
  4. Reasons: reason codes and customer comments are sorted into themes — too small, too large, size/fit, damaged/faulty, not as described, poor quality, wrong item, late, changed mind. The code wins; the comment fills gaps and tells "too small" from "too large".
  5. Size signal: a product "runs small" when at least 5 returns say small or large and 65%+ of them say small (and the other way for large).
  6. Fix or delist: products are ranked by total return cost. A delist suggestion needs a return rate of at least 30% and 1.75× your store average, with faults or quality as the main reason.
  7. Frequent returners: customers with 3+ returned items and at least half of what they bought sent back.

Limits: refunds are not all lost money — you usually get the item back — so the "cost" figure is the cash that left, not lost profit. Under Australian Consumer Law customers are entitled to a remedy for faulty items whatever your policy says. Frequent returners are a prompt for a conversation, not proof of abuse.
