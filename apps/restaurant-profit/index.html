Enter what you buy: the pack size and price on the invoice, and the yield % — how much is usable after trimming, peeling or cooking loss (e.g. lettuce 70%, salmon fillet 92%). Density (grams per ml) is only needed if a recipe measures a solid in cups or spoons, or a liquid in grams.

CSV columns: name, supplier, packSize, packUnit, packPrice, yieldPct, density.

Enter how many of each dish you sold over the same period (e.g. last 4 weeks, from your POS report), or import a CSV with columns dish, qty. Each dish is then placed by popularity and by contribution margin (price ex GST minus plate cost).

Theoretical food cost is what you should have used, worked out from dishes sold (Menu engineering tab) and your recipes. Actual is what left the cool room: opening stock + purchases − closing stock. A big gap usually means over-portioning, unrecorded waste, or theft. Count in each ingredient’s pack unit (e.g. kg).

Menu prices you enter are GST-inclusive (what the customer pays). Food cost % is worked out on the price without GST.
